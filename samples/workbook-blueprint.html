1. Operational flow

Order lines + discount policy → line gross → line discount → line net → net goods total + freight → quoted total

The quantity threshold applies to each line. It does not add all quantities together to determine discount eligibility. Freight is determined after discounts. These two details should remain visible in the replacement specification.

4. Proposed replacement data model

These entities are design proposals, not objects found inside the workbook.

  • Quote: identifier, status, currency, line collection, applied policy version, net goods total, freight and total. Customer identity and quote expiry require clarification.
  • Quote line: SKU, quantity, unit price, gross, discount rate, discount amount and net amount. Keep derived amounts separate from editable inputs.
  • Pricing policy: discount quantity, discount rate, free-freight threshold and freight charge. Confirm who can change policy and whether old quotes retain their original policy.

6. Draft acceptance examples

The first four preserve observed behavior. The remaining cases require a business decision before an expected result can be approved.

  1. Quantity boundary: a line with quantity 9 and unit price USD 80 has gross/net USD 720 and zero discount. At quantity 10, gross is USD 800, discount USD 80 and net USD 720.
  2. Freight boundary: net goods exactly USD 1,000 gives freight USD 0. Net goods USD 999.99 gives freight USD 45.
  3. Per-line eligibility: two lines of five units do not receive a discount merely because total units equal ten.
  4. Current reconciliation: the supplied three lines give net goods USD 1,220, freight USD 0 and total USD 1,220.
  5. Rounding decision: confirm approved outcomes for fractional unit prices before codifying cents precision.
  6. Validation decision: decide whether negative quantities/prices are rejected or treated as returns.
  7. Policy version decision: decide whether a saved quote changes when the active pricing policy is edited.
